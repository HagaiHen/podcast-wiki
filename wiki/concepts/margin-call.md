# Margin Call

**Summary:** When you invest with money borrowed from your broker, the broker protects its loan: if your portfolio falls below the required collateral ratio (e.g. a ~30% drop when half the money is borrowed), it demands a deposit within hours or force-sells your holdings. Forced sales lock in losses at the worst moment and, at scale, deepen the crash itself.

## Key ideas
- The broker's stance: "I won't lose on you." Below the collateral threshold you must top up, often the same day, or face a forced sale of all holdings ([[episodes/lazy-investing--108-the-leverage-trap]], [[people/tamir]]).
- Tens of thousands of South Korean investors got the same notice in 2026; the illustrative investor was liquidated at 3:30pm, left with $600 of $45k ([[episodes/lazy-investing--108-the-leverage-trap]]).
- Forced sellers' shares are bought cheaply by patient, often passive investors ([[episodes/lazy-investing--108-the-leverage-trap]], quoting [[people/warren-buffett]]).
