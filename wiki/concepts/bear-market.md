# Bear Market (living through a 20% drawdown)

**Summary:** A bear market is a fall of 20% or more from the last all-time high. On November 20, 2026 the Nasdaq crossed that line after a 5.47% day, which left it negative for the year and erased most of the AI trade's gains. Micha Stocks doesn't claim to know where the bottom is. Most of the fall is probably behind it, since the next technical target is only about 3 points lower, and a return to the high would mean roughly 25% upside, so for him it was worth buying. The signal he wants is a strong green week in which buyers take back control; another negative week would hurt the chance of a positive year. Bear markets are also when unprepared investors get exposed. His rules for staying in are stop-losses, a long horizon, a diversified index such as the S&P 500 for most people, and zero leverage unless you really know what you're doing. "In a bull market you make money; in a bear market you get rich."

## Key ideas
- The November 20, 2026 day: Nasdaq −5.47% (−20% from its high, a bear market), S&P 500 −3.5%, Russell −2.71%. Nvidia fell 13.8%, AMD and Micron about 13%, SanDisk 16.3% (about 73% below its high), while Microsoft (−2%), Amazon (−4%) and Apple held up better ([[episodes/micha-stocks--nasdaq-bear-market]], [[people/micha-stocks]]).
- In context: one of the worst Nasdaq weeks of the past 20 years, alongside COVID, the 2008 financial crisis, 2018 and the 2025 tariff tantrum. Year to date the Nasdaq turned negative and the S&P 500 was about flat, "a year erased" ([[episodes/micha-stocks--nasdaq-bear-market]], [[people/micha-stocks]]).
- Is the AI trade over? Micha's answer is no: companies are still buying and selling AI and making money from it, so he reads the fall as an overreaction ([[episodes/micha-stocks--nasdaq-bear-market]], [[people/micha-stocks]]).
- Most of the drop is probably done: an open gap on QQQ near 588 is about −22.8% from the high against −20% now, so even a further fall is small relative to what already happened. That doesn't mean the falls are over ([[episodes/micha-stocks--nasdaq-bear-market]], [[people/micha-stocks]]).
- Upside math over bottom-calling: buying at −20% "isn't fun", but if you assume the Nasdaq returns to its high, that's ~25% potential, which is why he bought, while saying he has no idea whether it's the bottom and that buying may not suit everyone ([[episodes/micha-stocks--nasdaq-bear-market]], [[people/micha-stocks]]).
- What would signal a turn: a strong green week, not one that recovers everything, but one where buyers regain dominance. Another negative week would weaken the case for ending the year positive, which he still expects ([[episodes/micha-stocks--nasdaq-bear-market]], [[people/micha-stocks]]).
- "When the tide goes out you see who's swimming naked": people who traded without stops or with leveraged positions lost fortunes, and many wrote to him. Their stories usually start with "I went in, didn't set a stop, and now I'm down." Recovering from −70% is very hard, maybe impossible ([[episodes/micha-stocks--nasdaq-bear-market]], [[people/micha-stocks]]).
- His rules for anyone still in the market: use stops (Instagram voices mocked them, "and we saw what not needing stops means"); look at the long term, not the short; for most people an S&P 500 index, which is more diversified (he takes more growth and volatility himself); cut leverage to zero unless you master the market ([[episodes/micha-stocks--nasdaq-bear-market]], [[people/micha-stocks]]).

## Disagreements & open questions
- Stops in every market? Here Micha says to always use them; in a narrow, sideways market he noted that stops keep getting hit and advised trading less ([[episodes/micha-stocks--index-bull-portfolio-bear]], [[episodes/micha-stocks--nasdaq-bear-market]], [[people/micha-stocks]]). Both fit a trader's view; a buy-and-hold index investor wouldn't use stops at all ([[concepts/index-investing]]).
- Buying the drawdown is itself a bet on recovery. Micha bought at −20% while saying the bottom can't be known; Tamir says only horizon and volatility tolerance should drive decisions ([[episodes/micha-stocks--nasdaq-bear-market]], [[episodes/lazy-investing--111-the-ai-bubble]]). See [[concepts/market-timing]].

## Takeaways
- [ ] Before adding to a position in a bear market, write down the upside to the previous high and the next downside level, and size for being wrong ([[episodes/micha-stocks--nasdaq-bear-market]])
- [ ] If you trade single stocks, set a stop-loss on every position when you enter it ([[episodes/micha-stocks--nasdaq-bear-market]])
- [ ] In a drawdown, wait for a strong green week with buyers in control before reading it as a turn ([[episodes/micha-stocks--nasdaq-bear-market]])
