# Circuit Breakers (why an index can't crash 22% in a day)

**Summary:** After Black Monday (19 October 1987, Dow −22.6% in one day), US exchanges introduced market-wide circuit breakers that halt trading when the S&P 500 falls 7% or 13% intraday and close the market at −20%. A one-day index collapse like 1987 is therefore practically impossible today, and you'd have time to place a sell order. Big index declines still happen, just spread over days: the 2020 COVID crash took the S&P 500 down 35% over 23 trading days. Single stocks have no such protection, which is one reason indexes exist. Viral "bloody Monday" posts trade on fear for views.

## Key ideas
- Rules today: S&P 500 −7% → 15-minute halt; −13% → another 15-minute halt; −20% → trading stops for the day. They were set by a task force formed in January 1988 and updated over time ([[episodes/micha-stocks--bloody-monday-circuit-breakers]], [[people/micha-stocks]]).
- COVID 2020: the S&P 500 fell from 3,393 to 2,191 (−35%) across 23 trading days; the first −12% took six sessions of −0.4% to −4.4% each ([[episodes/micha-stocks--bloody-monday-circuit-breakers]], [[people/micha-stocks]]).
- A 22% overnight drop is near zero for an index, but a single company can do it on bad earnings or company news ([[episodes/micha-stocks--bloody-monday-circuit-breakers]], [[people/micha-stocks]]).
- Crash posts spread when the Fear & Greed index is at extreme fear and "every touch makes nerves jump"; their authors are paid per view ([[episodes/micha-stocks--bloody-monday-circuit-breakers]], [[people/micha-stocks]]).
- The predicted bloodbath didn't come: that Monday the Nasdaq closed +1.2%, the S&P 500 +0.42% ([[episodes/micha-stocks--bloody-monday-circuit-breakers]], [[people/micha-stocks]]).
