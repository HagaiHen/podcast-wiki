# Index Investing

**Summary:** Buying broad, diversified, global stock indexes (e.g. the S&P 500) and holding long term. Historically, every major drawdown in broad indexes has eventually recovered, which is why patient, unleveraged investors can ride out crashes and even buy more. It's "slow getting-rich", and it still requires some basic learning first.

## Key ideas
- Broad, diversified indexes have recovered from every significant decline so far ([[episodes/lazy-investing--108-the-leverage-trap]], [[people/tamir]]).
- Before investing, spend roughly 3–10 hours learning: your volatility tolerance, emergency fund, which index, equity exposure, which ETF, management fees, time horizon, and goals ([[episodes/lazy-investing--108-the-leverage-trap]]).
- Most people don't hold a portfolio for five years; those who jump in because a friend profited are the ones who lose (host's claim) ([[episodes/lazy-investing--108-the-leverage-trap]]).
- The market is "a mechanism for transferring money from the impatient to the patient" ([[people/warren-buffett]], quoted in [[episodes/lazy-investing--108-the-leverage-trap]]).
