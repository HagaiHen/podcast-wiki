# Index Investing

**Summary:** Buying broad, diversified, global stock indexes (e.g. the S&P 500) and holding long term. Historically, every major drawdown in broad indexes has eventually recovered, which is why patient, unleveraged investors can ride out crashes, even welcome them as a chance to buy more. Diversification across companies and sectors is the only free lunch, and a broad index captures each technology wave (internet, AI) through all the companies that adopt it, not just the hyped ones. It's "slow getting-rich", and it still requires some basic learning first.

## Key ideas
- Broad, diversified indexes have recovered from every significant decline so far ([[episodes/lazy-investing--108-the-leverage-trap]], [[people/tamir]]).
- Before investing, spend roughly 3–10 hours learning: your volatility tolerance, emergency fund, which index, equity exposure, which ETF, management fees, time horizon, and goals ([[episodes/lazy-investing--108-the-leverage-trap]]).
- Most people don't hold a portfolio for five years; those who jump in because a friend profited are the ones who lose (host's claim) ([[episodes/lazy-investing--108-the-leverage-trap]]).
- The market is "a mechanism for transferring money from the impatient to the patient" ([[people/warren-buffett]], quoted in [[episodes/lazy-investing--108-the-leverage-trap]]).
- Diversify across companies and sectors, including boring ones; they benefit from new technology too ([[episodes/lazy-investing--111-the-ai-bubble]], [[people/tamir]]).
- One of the reasons to invest in indexes is that they rise with technological progress; the index would have risen without AI, just less ([[episodes/lazy-investing--111-the-ai-bubble]], [[people/tamir]]).
- The host doesn't sell his holdings over bubble fears and recommends against picking individual stocks ([[episodes/lazy-investing--111-the-ai-bubble]], [[people/tamir]]).
