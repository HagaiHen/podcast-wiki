# AI Work Redesign (reinventing work around AI)

**Summary:** Adding agents to existing processes saves hours, but the larger value may come from rebuilding the work itself around what AI can do. monday.com's finance AI lead describes three stages: get nearly everyone using AI, build many agents on existing processes, then pick one area and redesign it as if building it today. Each stage prepares the next: teams need daily AI habits before they can accept agents, and they need working agents before their role can change. The trigger for stage three was a department leader asking where all the disconnected agents were heading, which he couldn't answer. This is one company's self-reported experience, and the first redesigned process hadn't yet reached its full team, so its results are still open.

## Key ideas
- Stage 1, adoption: without people using AI daily, you can't go deeper to agents. Adoption is a proxy for value and prepares the ground; it isn't value in itself ([[episodes/startup-for-startup--3-stages-of-ai-adoption]], [[people/dror-adi]]).
- Stage 2, build: with adoption near 100%, take the roadmap and build, starting with low-effort, high-impact tasks outside SOX compliance scope, to show value fast ([[episodes/startup-for-startup--3-stages-of-ai-adoption]], [[people/dror-adi]]).
- Stage 2 examples: an agent reads a shared finance mailbox, extracts key details, finds the right representative in company systems and routes the request in real time, ending daily human shifts on that inbox. Another chases employees over Slack and email for finance follow-ups, a task said to cost hundreds of hours a month ([[episodes/startup-for-startup--3-stages-of-ai-adoption]], [[people/dror-adi]]).
- Prioritize by more than impact: map each pain to estimated hours saved, risk, impact, SOX relevance, reuse across teams, and whether the team has a champion. He calls the champion score the "Limor metric", after Limor Mizrahi, a colleague in the department who pushed hard for AI ([[episodes/startup-for-startup--3-stages-of-ai-adoption]], [[people/dror-adi]]).
- Why finance is hard: a public company needs controls and accuracy, privacy concerns are serious, and dozens of global teams (FP&A, revenue, tax, collections) do entirely different work, so one broad solution rarely fits ([[episodes/startup-for-startup--3-stages-of-ai-adoption]], [[people/dror-adi]]).
- The turning point: a department leader, Moran, asked "where is this going?" Connected MCPs and agents were scattered, with hours saved but no connecting thread ([[episodes/startup-for-startup--3-stages-of-ai-adoption]], [[people/dror-adi]]).
- Industrial-revolution analogy, heard from a speaker at the Ai4 conference and not checked further: factories installed new machines inside old workflows and saw almost no productivity gain for 40 years, until work was reorganized around the machines ([[episodes/startup-for-startup--3-stages-of-ai-adoption]], [[people/dror-adi]]).
- Stage 3, reinvent: stop the agent sprawl, choose one area with management, and ask how it should look five years from now, or if built today with everything known about the technology ([[episodes/startup-for-startup--3-stages-of-ai-adoption]], [[people/dror-adi]]).
- Choosing the area: collections, because the global team is large, it turns signed contracts into cash, the work is operational across about eight platforms, and existing agents already showed it was AI-ready ([[episodes/startup-for-startup--3-stages-of-ai-adoption]], [[people/dror-adi]]).
- The goal is a changed role: from high-volume operations to strategic, proactive work focused on the important customers and largest sums ([[episodes/startup-for-startup--3-stages-of-ai-adoption]], [[people/dror-adi]]).
- The redesigned flow: on a customer email or open process, an agent gathers data across systems, including churn risk and open balance, and computes a new prioritization score. It says where it can handle a case with the rep's approval and where the rep is needed, so people stop assembling dozens of data points by hand ([[episodes/startup-for-startup--3-stages-of-ai-adoption]], [[people/dror-adi]]).
- The knowledge sits with the team: he says he doesn't and won't have their finance expertise, so they shape decisions. The work included neighboring pains (e.g. with sales), and one rep iterated closely with him until the agent's answers, decisions and customer wording earned trust ([[episodes/startup-for-startup--3-stages-of-ai-adoption]], [[people/dror-adi]]).
- Cadence: one area at a time, apparently per quarter (the transcript is unclear here). After money coming in (collections) comes money going out (accounts payable) ([[episodes/startup-for-startup--3-stages-of-ai-adoption]], [[people/dror-adi]]).
- For a small company with no dedicated lead: find the person already building things on their own, back them, give them product skills such as user interviews, and make progress data visible ([[episodes/startup-for-startup--3-stages-of-ai-adoption]], [[people/dror-adi]]).

## Disagreements & open questions
- Does redesign beat incremental automation? The case for it here rests on an analogy and a rollout that was only approaching global launch, with no outcome numbers yet ([[episodes/startup-for-startup--3-stages-of-ai-adoption]], [[people/dror-adi]]).

## Takeaways
- [ ] Score each AI opportunity on hours saved, risk, impact, compliance scope, reuse across teams, and whether a champion exists ([[episodes/startup-for-startup--3-stages-of-ai-adoption]])
- [ ] Start agent building with low-effort tasks outside compliance scope, then move toward regulated work ([[episodes/startup-for-startup--3-stages-of-ai-adoption]])
- [ ] Pick one high-impact area and sketch how you'd build it today from scratch, rather than adding another agent to the current process ([[episodes/startup-for-startup--3-stages-of-ai-adoption]])
