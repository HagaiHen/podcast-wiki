# Key-Person Risk

**Summary:** Key-person risk is how much a company's value rests on one individual, usually a founder-CEO, and what happens to the stock if that person suddenly goes. The November 2026 Nvidia case shows how it plays out: reports about Jensen Huang's personal relationships, one of them with an executive who reported to him, led to talk that the board might fire him, and the stock fell about 8% after hours. The problem is continuity: a successor has to be found under pressure, and the market prices in that doubt immediately. The open question is how much of a stock's drop is really about the person. The same host shows that Nvidia's chart looks a lot like Google's, whose CEO did nothing, which suggests part of the "scandal" selloff is an ordinary correction with a story attached.

## Key ideas
- The trigger: days after reports that Jensen Huang had an extramarital relationship with a woman suspected of being a Chinese spy (which he acknowledged in an email to employees), the New York Times reported a second relationship with a senior executive who reported to him. The board was reportedly about to convene and possibly fire him ([[episodes/micha-stocks--breaking-nvidia-ceo]], [[people/micha-stocks]]).
- A relationship with a direct report is something a board finds very hard to let stand, "even if it's Jensen Huang" ([[episodes/micha-stocks--breaking-nvidia-ceo]], [[people/micha-stocks]]).
- The market reacts before the decision: Nvidia fell about 7.8% after hours, below $160, while the board was still meeting ([[episodes/micha-stocks--breaking-nvidia-ceo]], [[people/micha-stocks]]).
- The core issue is continuity. Everyone compares it to Apple replacing Steve Jobs, but Jobs left for different reasons (he died), so the precedent only partly fits ([[episodes/micha-stocks--breaking-nvidia-ceo]], [[people/micha-stocks]]).
- Headline risk stacks up around a company like this: Nvidia was also caught in a fight between the US Treasury and Commerce secretaries over a Singapore company and chip transfers, so "every headline can move the stock" ([[episodes/micha-stocks--breaking-nvidia-ceo]], [[people/micha-stocks]]).

## Disagreements & open questions
- Person or correction? Micha says losing Huang would be a hard continuity problem, yet on the same day overlays Nvidia's chart on Google's and suggests the drop may be an ordinary correction, with the relationship only the explanation attached to it ([[episodes/micha-stocks--breaking-nvidia-ceo]], [[people/micha-stocks]]).

## Takeaways
- [ ] For any single stock you hold, ask what happens if the CEO leaves tomorrow, and size the position for that case ([[episodes/micha-stocks--breaking-nvidia-ceo]])
