# AI Growth Marketing (agentic growth hacking)

**Summary:** AI isn't just automating existing marketing; it opens channels humans can't work at scale. Agents can read whole subreddits, find gaps in Wikipedia, hit platform ranking windows, and hyper-personalize messages for thousands of micro-segments. Autonomous agents act as a *thinking multiplier*: they iterate experiments (copy, offers, outreach paths) and report what worked. Cheap assets change the playbook too: launch videos that once cost six figures, free calculators as lead magnets, and "permissionless" outreach where you send the finished work instead of a cold email. Today this succeeds in organic and unconventional channels, SEO/GEO, and SDR agents: monday's sales agents book thousands of meetings a month by treating each lead as an individual rather than a segment. Scarcity still works: Instinct AI's invite-only rollout cut through the flood of "introducing X agent" launches. Paid ads and huge-context enterprise outreach don't work yet. Many tactics lean on platform gray areas.

## Key ideas
- New marketing, not 10× old marketing: hunt "distribution loopholes" the way security people hunt vulnerabilities ([[episodes/ignore-instructions--28-ai-marketing-enso]], [[people/micky-haslavsky]]).
- Examples: monitoring Reddit across many subreddits; adding sourced content to Wikipedia gaps; LinkedIn's algorithm decides virality in the first ~18–60 minutes, and agent-driven engagement from many groups lifted posts from ~500 to tens of thousands of views ([[episodes/ignore-instructions--28-ai-marketing-enso]]).
- Scripted agentic workflows (≈99% of production agents) versus autonomous agents that learn. Marketing is iterative, so autonomy is where the power is: "try batches of 10 until conversion, then tell me what you learned" ([[episodes/ignore-instructions--28-ai-marketing-enso]]).
- Outsourcing *thinking*: an OpenClaw agent with web search, email, and phone tools tried paths for days to reach Ray Dalio, then contacted an alumni network the host never knew existed ([[episodes/ignore-instructions--28-ai-marketing-enso]]).
- Failures: an AI SDR startup's promise broke on LinkedIn API limits and context exhaustion; paid ads are blocked by video quality, iteration cost, and platforms' reluctance ([[episodes/ignore-instructions--28-ai-marketing-enso]]).
- Personalization: "a company with 1,000 GTMs", per-person newsletters, and outreach informed by deep research, which raises conversion and feels valuable rather than spammy ([[episodes/ignore-instructions--28-ai-marketing-enso]]).
- Model: Forward Deployed Marketers plus hundreds of agents, against the old ~5 people per client ([[episodes/ignore-instructions--28-ai-marketing-enso]]).
- Other uses: an adaptive interview agent screened 300+ candidates in a morning; Manus for research; no AI on finances ("bad at numbers") ([[episodes/ignore-instructions--28-ai-marketing-enso]]).
- Copywriting tip: feed real performance data (likes, comments, reach) back so the AI learns what works, rather than relying on taste ([[episodes/ignore-instructions--28-ai-marketing-enso]]).
- When software gets 10× cheaper you hear about 10× more products; distribution, not building, is the bottleneck ([[episodes/ignore-instructions--20-idan-benyon-build-ship-grow]], [[people/idan-benyon]]).
- Build in public: Idan grew from 2k to 13k LinkedIn followers in six months writing about his daily building, which brought first users. Talking about internal go-to-market tools attracts investors and customers ([[episodes/ignore-instructions--20-idan-benyon-build-ship-grow]], [[people/idan-benyon]]).
- AI content engines: small teams auto-generate natural-looking TikTok videos (e.g. motion-graphics skills, AI avatars), post across several accounts, plug their app every few posts, and run 3–4 apps through the engine to see what catches. One three-to-four-person team makes hundreds of thousands of dollars a year ([[episodes/ignore-instructions--20-idan-benyon-build-ship-grow]], [[people/idan-benyon]]).
- Open source as distribution: Postiz went open source in late 2024 and reached ~$50k MRR. When copying is easy, giving it away so everyone builds on top of you may be the moat; Chinese open-weight labs use the same logic ([[episodes/ignore-instructions--20-idan-benyon-build-ship-grow]], [[people/idan-benyon]], [[people/nevo-david]]).
- Reddit gives honest feedback from strangers, and posting there is less exposing than to your own network ([[episodes/ignore-instructions--20-idan-benyon-build-ship-grow]]).
- Launch videos: funded startups spend $100–300k on launch videos; the Brag skill makes a short one from a project or URL (tone, aspect ratio, thumbnail, post copy, per-scene redo) for model cost only ([[episodes/startup-ideas--8-github-repos-viral-money]], [[people/greg-isenberg]]).
- Permissionless outreach: instead of a cold email, do the work and send it, e.g. a launch video for a Product Hunt launch that has none, or a few concrete fixes to a confusing pricing page. At worst it starts a conversation that leads to other work ([[episodes/startup-ideas--8-github-repos-viral-money]], [[people/greg-isenberg]]).
- Free tools as lead magnets: a packaging supplier's box-quantity cost calculator attracts the right buyers, and a quote request then arrives already qualified. Show the result before asking for an email, then turn the estimate into a quote without re-entry ([[episodes/startup-ideas--8-github-repos-viral-money]], [[people/greg-isenberg]]).
- Customer language is copy: a complaint like "payroll takes my entire Friday" is a ready-made headline if your product solves it ([[episodes/startup-ideas--8-github-repos-viral-money]], [[people/greg-isenberg]]).
- Localized onboarding: voice-cloning and dubbing tools (e.g. the local, open-source Voice Studio) can turn English tutorials into other languages, with a fluent speaker checking. Start with the three questions support hears most. Clone only voices you have permission to use and check the license ([[episodes/startup-ideas--8-github-repos-viral-money]], [[people/greg-isenberg]]).

- monday's SDR agents: Amanda handles leads who asked to talk to sales, qualifies them and books a meeting with a human rep; Jax works new signups, helps them get value and spots commercial intent. Amanda gets a 35–45% meeting rate (about 2,500–2,700 meetings from ~7,000 leads a month), Jax hundreds of meetings, and sourced pipeline rose from $3–4M to $8.5M a month after the AgentOS platform ([[episodes/startup-for-startup--370-agentos-month-to-day]], [[people/moshe-krispin]], [[people/raz-diskin]])
- The unit of automation moves from segment or cohort to the individual: no two leads get the same treatment. Leads often ask whether they're talking to a human, and the agent has to say it's an AI ([[episodes/startup-for-startup--370-agentos-month-to-day]], [[people/raz-diskin]], [[people/moshe-krispin]])
- In sales, where personal connection matters most, agents have an edge: they remember. Six months later an agent knows to phone Jacob at 9 a.m. instead of emailing him ([[episodes/startup-for-startup--370-agentos-month-to-day]], [[people/moshe-krispin]])

- Invite-only launch: amid endless agent launches, Remy says he wouldn't have signed up if Instinct AI were open; being locked out made an invite feel special. The stated reason is compute, but it doubles as marketing, like Clubhouse in 2020. Features also roll out gradually to subsets of users ([[episodes/startup-ideas--instinct-ai-assistant-for-normal-people]], [[people/remy]], [[people/greg-isenberg]]).

## Disagreements & open questions
- Host vs guest: the host keeps iterating copy with an AI collaborator; Haslavsky suspects copy barely matters to virality versus timing and early engagement ([[episodes/ignore-instructions--28-ai-marketing-enso]]).

## Takeaways
- [ ] Give an autonomous agent an outcome (e.g. reach a person, find converting copy) plus tools, and ask it to report what it learned ([[episodes/ignore-instructions--28-ai-marketing-enso]])
- [ ] Feed post performance data back into your AI copywriting loop ([[episodes/ignore-instructions--28-ai-marketing-enso]])
- [ ] Build in public: post weekly about what you build day to day ([[episodes/ignore-instructions--20-idan-benyon-build-ship-grow]])
- [ ] Make a 20-second launch video for something you built (e.g. with Brag) and share it ([[episodes/startup-ideas--8-github-repos-viral-money]])
- [ ] Build one free calculator or tool for your audience; show the result before asking for an email ([[episodes/startup-ideas--8-github-repos-viral-money]])
