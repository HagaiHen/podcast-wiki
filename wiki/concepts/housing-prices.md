# Housing Prices (interest rates, growth, and investor demand)

**Summary:** Housing prices depend on demand as much as supply, and much of the demand comes from investors buying second apartments, who compare rental yields with other investments. Yaron Zelicha's view: when the business sector grows fast and interest rates rise with it, investors leave housing for better returns and young buyers have the market to themselves; when rates lag growth (Israel 2022), housing booms; when rates sit above near-zero growth (2026), the situation can't last and a rate cut will start the next boom. The policy lesson he draws is coordination between the government and the central bank so rates track growth. Micha Stocks pushes back: with 60–80k unsold new apartments there's no supply shortage, and more disposable income or growth (as in the post-COVID tech boom) has historically pushed prices up.

## Key ideas
- Demand has two parts: ~40–50k young first-time buyers a year, and investors for whom an apartment competes with alternative investments ([[episodes/micha-stocks--yaron-zelicha-economic-interview]], [[people/yaron-zelicha]]).
- 2003–2007: growth of ~3.5–4% per capita (business sector 6–7% a year) plus rising rates made a ~2.5% rental yield unattractive, so investors left; prices fell and the share of 27-year-olds owning a home rose from 35% to 51% ([[episodes/micha-stocks--yaron-zelicha-economic-interview]], [[people/yaron-zelicha]]).
- 2022: rates lagged the post-COVID growth spurt; he says he called for hikes early in 2022, and housing rose ~22% within about 15 months ([[episodes/micha-stocks--yaron-zelicha-economic-interview]], [[people/yaron-zelicha]]).
- Principle: no gap between growth and the interest rate, within the bounds of a Taylor-type rule; the government and Bank of Israel must coordinate. In 2026 the real rate exceeds near-zero underlying growth, which can't hold; when rates fall, "the boom will start" ([[episodes/micha-stocks--yaron-zelicha-economic-interview]], [[people/yaron-zelicha]]).
- Against stretching mortgages to 40–50 years; mortgage competition is relatively good and not the core problem ([[episodes/micha-stocks--yaron-zelicha-economic-interview]], [[people/yaron-zelicha]]).
- Supply isn't the bottleneck: per Finance Ministry figures there are ~60–80k unsold new apartments (~50–60k ready, the rest under construction); young people want to buy but price plus mortgage cost is out of reach ([[episodes/micha-stocks--yaron-zelicha-economic-interview]], [[people/micha-stocks]]).
- Israeli tech and real estate are tightly linked: the post-COVID tech boom brought in money that drove dramatic price rises ([[episodes/micha-stocks--yaron-zelicha-economic-interview]], [[people/micha-stocks]]).
- Supply-side ideas from another candidate: incentives for long-term rental construction, neighborhood-scale urban renewal, and incentives for mayors (see [[concepts/cost-of-living]]) ([[episodes/micha-stocks--naftali-bennett-economic-interview]], [[people/naftali-bennett]]).

## Disagreements & open questions
- Does growth raise or lower housing prices? Micha: higher disposable income and growth create inflation and push housing up. Zelicha: only when the rate lags growth; growth with matching rates diverts investors away from housing ([[episodes/micha-stocks--yaron-zelicha-economic-interview]], [[people/micha-stocks]], [[people/yaron-zelicha]]).
