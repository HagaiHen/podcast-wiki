# Compound Interest

**Summary:** Returns earning returns over time: the engine of "slow getting-rich". Young investors who give up on long-term saving because a home feels out of reach miss that compounding rewards starting early more than taking big risks. Small differences in annual return become huge over decades: 5.6% versus the ~11% nominal that stocks returned over the past 30 years is a large gap for a young investor.

## Key ideas
- Young South Koreans abandoned long-term saving, believing they'd never afford a home, and chased leverage instead. They underestimated compounding ([[episodes/lazy-investing--108-the-leverage-trap]], [[people/tamir]]).
- Dream of slow wealth via broad indexes, not quick wealth via leverage or stock-picking ([[episodes/lazy-investing--108-the-leverage-trap]]).
- Over 30 years, the gap between 5.6% (today's US bond yield) and ~11% (historical nominal stock returns) is "a lot of money" ([[episodes/lazy-investing--111-the-ai-bubble]], [[people/tamir]]).
