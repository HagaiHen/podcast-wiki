# Leverage (Investing)

**Summary:** Leverage means investing borrowed money on top of your own: a broker margin loan, a loan from another account, or a leveraged fund that delivers x2–x5 of an index's move. "Same lady, different dress": every form multiplies gains *and* losses, and every form removes your control over when you exit. With a margin loan the broker force-sells once your collateral runs out (a margin call). With a leveraged ETF, a 20% drop in the underlying wipes out a x5 fund, and a normal 50% crash takes any leveraged fund to roughly zero. The 2026 South Korean crash is the cautionary case: one-button x2–x5 leverage inflated a few stocks, then forced liquidations turned a sharp fall into wiped-out accounts.

## Key ideas
- Doubling exposure doubles returns: invest $45k + $45k borrowed, a 10% gain earns $9k instead of $4.5k ([[episodes/lazy-investing--108-the-leverage-trap]], [[people/tamir]]).
- With an unleveraged broad index, you decide when to leave; it only goes to zero if essentially every company shuts down. With leverage, the broker or fund decides for you ([[episodes/lazy-investing--108-the-leverage-trap]]).
- Normal crashes of ~40–50% (2000, 2008, 2020) are survivable unleveraged (you can hold or buy more) but trigger margin calls or near-total losses when leveraged ([[episodes/lazy-investing--108-the-leverage-trap]]).
- "Same lady, different dress": margin, borrowed money, and leveraged ETFs all carry the same risk ([[episodes/lazy-investing--108-the-leverage-trap]]).
- In South Korea, brokers let retail investors pick x2–x5 leverage on Samsung, SK Hynix, or the KOSPI with one button, which normalized it ([[episodes/lazy-investing--108-the-leverage-trap]]).

### Margin calls (borrowing from the broker)
- The broker's stance: "I won't lose on you." If the portfolio falls below the required collateral ratio (e.g. a ~30% drop when half the money is borrowed), you must top up, often the same day, or face a forced sale of all holdings ([[episodes/lazy-investing--108-the-leverage-trap]], [[people/tamir]]).
- Tens of thousands of South Korean investors got the same notice in 2026; the illustrative investor was liquidated at 3:30pm, left with $600 of $45k ([[episodes/lazy-investing--108-the-leverage-trap]]).
- Forced sales lock in losses at the worst moment and, at scale, deepen the crash itself. Forced sellers' shares are bought cheaply by patient, often passive investors ([[episodes/lazy-investing--108-the-leverage-trap]], quoting [[people/warren-buffett]]).

### Leveraged ETFs (borrowing inside a fund)
- A x5 chip ETF up 10% in a week means +50% for you: very shareable on social media. But a 20% fall erases the whole investment ([[episodes/lazy-investing--108-the-leverage-trap]], [[people/tamir]]).
- Expect ~50% index drops about once a decade historically; with leveraged funds, expect ~80% drops several times a decade, possibly total loss ([[episodes/lazy-investing--108-the-leverage-trap]]).
- "Leverage the index, since indexes win long term" fails because you lose control over when you exit ([[episodes/lazy-investing--108-the-leverage-trap]]).
- Daily-reset leverage adds further decay (mentioned, not explained) ([[episodes/lazy-investing--108-the-leverage-trap]]).
