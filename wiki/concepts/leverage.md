# Leverage (Investing)

**Summary:** Leverage means investing borrowed money on top of your own: via a broker margin loan, a loan from another account, or a leveraged fund. It multiplies gains *and* losses, and crucially it removes your control over when you exit: a large enough drop forces a sale at the bottom. The 2026 South Korean crash is the cautionary case: easy leverage inflated a few stocks, then forced liquidations turned a sharp fall into wiped-out accounts.

## Key ideas
- Doubling exposure doubles returns: invest $45k + $45k borrowed, a 10% gain earns $9k instead of $4.5k ([[episodes/lazy-investing--108-the-leverage-trap]], [[people/tamir]]).
- With an unleveraged broad index, you decide when to leave; it only goes to zero if essentially every company shuts down. With leverage, the broker or fund decides for you ([[episodes/lazy-investing--108-the-leverage-trap]]).
- Normal crashes of ~40–50% (2000, 2008, 2020) are survivable unleveraged (you can hold or buy more) but trigger [[concepts/margin-call]]s or near-total losses when leveraged ([[episodes/lazy-investing--108-the-leverage-trap]]).
- "Same lady, different dress": margin, borrowed money, and [[concepts/leveraged-etfs]] all carry the same risk ([[episodes/lazy-investing--108-the-leverage-trap]]).
- In South Korea, brokers let retail investors pick x2–x5 leverage on Samsung, SK Hynix, or the KOSPI with one button, which normalized it ([[episodes/lazy-investing--108-the-leverage-trap]]).
