# Market Breadth

**Summary:** Market breadth asks how many stocks take part in a move, not just where the index ends up. A cap-weighted index like the S&P 500 can sit within 5% of its all-time high while most of its stocks are in a correction or a bear market, because a handful of giants carry it. That gap is why an index investor can feel fine while a stock picker's portfolio is deep red. The usual way to see it is to compare the cap-weighted S&P 500 with its equal-weight version (RSP) and to count how many stocks are well below their highs.

## Key ideas
- In October 2026 the S&P 500 was <5% from its high, yet 84% of its stocks were 10%+ below their own highs and 59% were 20%+ below, i.e. in a bear market. By sector: real estate 97%, materials 96%, utilities 94%, consumer discretionary 92%, staples 91%, financials 87% ([[episodes/micha-stocks--index-bull-portfolio-bear]], [[people/micha-stocks]]).
- 7 of 11 sectors were negative for the year while the index was up ~12%; only tech (+37%) and energy (+23%) beat it ([[episodes/micha-stocks--index-bull-portfolio-bear]], [[people/micha-stocks]]).
- Equal-weight vs cap-weight is the clearest breadth gauge: the S&P 500 was up 12.2% vs ~3.6–6% for equal-weight RSP, so the average stock didn't benefit from the rally ([[episodes/micha-stocks--index-bull-portfolio-bear]], [[people/micha-stocks]]).
- Narrow markets have happened before: in 2022–2023 a small group of companies also held the index up while the rest churned ([[episodes/micha-stocks--index-bull-portfolio-bear]], [[people/micha-stocks]]).
- A sideways, narrow market is a time to trade less: stops keep getting hit when there's no clear direction ([[episodes/micha-stocks--index-bull-portfolio-bear]], [[people/micha-stocks]]).

## Disagreements & open questions
- Whether "bear market" is the right word is a measurement question: specific sectors and stocks are in one, but the S&P 500 as a market is not ([[episodes/micha-stocks--index-bull-portfolio-bear]], [[people/micha-stocks]]).
