# Equity Risk Premium (Earnings Yield vs Bonds)

**Summary:** A stock market's earnings yield is 1 divided by its P/E: a P/E of 18.9 means about 5.3% a year in earnings per dollar invested. When safe government bonds pay about the same, valuation-driven investors see no reason to take stock risk, which can cap the market even while earnings grow fast. Index price is earnings times multiple, so the multiple matters as much as profits: if earnings jump and prices lag, the market got cheaper, and a small return of the premium can lift prices a lot. Rising bond yields are the main threat, because they squeeze that premium further.

## Key ideas
- With Treasuries around 5–5.2% and the S&P 500 at a P/E of 18.9 (earnings yield ~5.29%), you get the same return without risk, which is why the index is stuck in a 7,200–7,700 range ([[episodes/micha-stocks--index-bull-portfolio-bear]], [[people/micha-stocks]]).
- "The market follows earnings": earnings grew ~47% year over year (energy +135%, tech +69%) while the index rose only ~12%, so the multiple fell and the market got cheaper (host's figures) ([[episodes/micha-stocks--index-bull-portfolio-bear]], [[people/micha-stocks]]).
- Analyst targets are simple math: earnings × multiple. Moving the P/E from 18.9 back to ~22, where it was a few months earlier, gives an S&P 500 of ~9,000 ([[episodes/micha-stocks--index-bull-portfolio-bear]], [[people/micha-stocks]]).
- Tech's P/E is now roughly the S&P 500's, i.e. zero premium for tech despite the AI boom; even a small premium coming back would lift the market ([[episodes/micha-stocks--index-bull-portfolio-bear]], [[people/micha-stocks]]).
- With US Treasuries near 5.6%, stocks must beat that for decades to be worth it, which makes the market fragile if investors switch to bonds ([[episodes/lazy-investing--111-the-ai-bubble]], [[people/tamir]]).
- Macro shifted fast: in January 2026 rate cuts were expected; by October CPI was 3.7%, Fed funds 3.8%, 30-year mortgages 7.4%, the 30-year bond 5.56%, and CME FedWatch put ~87% odds on a December hike ([[episodes/micha-stocks--index-bull-portfolio-bear]], [[people/micha-stocks]]).

## Disagreements & open questions
- Cheap or expensive? Tamir points to a CAPE above 41 and a market yield of only ~1% ([[episodes/lazy-investing--111-the-ai-bubble]]); Micha uses a forward-looking P/E of 18.9 (~5.3% yield) and calls the market and tech cheap ([[episodes/micha-stocks--index-bull-portfolio-bear]]). Different measures (10-year average earnings vs current earnings) explain much of the gap. See [[concepts/cape-ratio]].
