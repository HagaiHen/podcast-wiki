# CAPE Ratio (Shiller P/E)

**Summary:** The Shiller cyclically adjusted P/E divides price by the average inflation-adjusted earnings of the last ten years, so a single unusual year (a war, a cold summer for an ice-cream shop) doesn't distort the valuation. A CAPE of 41 means investors pay $41 for each dollar of average annual earnings. High CAPE predicts lower future returns on average, but it is not a timing signal: markets can stay expensive for years. In October 2026 the S&P 500's CAPE was above 41, a level exceeded only during the dot-com bubble, while its ordinary P/E on current earnings was only ~18.9; when earnings grow fast, the two measures can tell opposite stories.

## Key ideas
- CAPE averages ten years of inflation-adjusted earnings, measuring value "over a long time" instead of one possibly abnormal year ([[episodes/lazy-investing--111-the-ai-bubble]], [[people/tamir]]).
- The only time the S&P 500's CAPE was higher than today's ~41 was the dot-com era ([[episodes/lazy-investing--111-the-ai-bubble]], [[people/tamir]]).
- Revenue isn't profit: revenues can double while a company loses money; CAPE looks at earnings ([[episodes/lazy-investing--111-the-ai-bubble]]).
- When prices are high, expected future returns should be lower, for stocks as for apartments ([[episodes/lazy-investing--111-the-ai-bubble]], [[people/tamir]]).
- Competing with bonds: with US Treasury yields around 5.6%, buying stocks is a bet that they'll beat 5.6% a year over decades, a tall promise when the market's yield is only ~1% (the host's figure). That makes the market fragile if investors switch to bonds ([[episodes/lazy-investing--111-the-ai-bubble]], [[people/tamir]]).
- Stocks still beat bonds long term for two reasons: companies pass inflation through to prices (a ₪55.4 sunscreen becomes ₪56.4), and they stay tied to whatever technology rally comes ([[episodes/lazy-investing--111-the-ai-bubble]], [[people/tamir]]).
- On current earnings the S&P 500's P/E was ~18.9 in October 2026, down from ~22 a few months earlier because earnings grew much faster than prices (host's figures) ([[episodes/micha-stocks--index-bull-portfolio-bear]], [[people/micha-stocks]]).

## Disagreements & open questions
- High valuation vs staying invested: CAPE says expect lower returns, but the host still won't sell, because acting on valuation has repeatedly cost investors (see [[concepts/market-timing]]) ([[episodes/lazy-investing--111-the-ai-bubble]]).
- Expensive vs cheap: CAPE ~41 says the market is near record expensive ([[episodes/lazy-investing--111-the-ai-bubble]]); Micha says a P/E of 18.9 and fast earnings growth make it cheap ([[episodes/micha-stocks--index-bull-portfolio-bear]]). See [[concepts/equity-risk-premium]].
