# Startup Exits (when and how to sell)

**Summary:** An exit is a timing decision as much as a price. Every round raised at a higher valuation removes buyers who can no longer afford you, and in a niche market (government 911 software, roughly ten possible acquirers, maybe five realistic) growing too big can leave only an IPO the company isn't ready for. Carbyne's founder ran both tracks at once: he announced a $100M raise through Goldman Sachs so potential buyers knew it was now or never, took the eventual acquirer (Axon) in as lead investor, and sold to it soon after for $625M in cash plus ~$100M in equity. His rule is a "win-win sell point": don't wait for the top of the chart, sell when the buyer still has room to create value. A strong fallback (keep growing on your own) is what gives you leverage in the negotiation.

## Key ideas
- Each raise erases buyers: a higher valuation means a buyer must offer even more, so some potential acquirers drop off the list ([[episodes/yasmins-living-room--amir-elichai-carbyne]], [[people/yasmin]], [[people/amir-elichai]]).
- In a small market the acquirer list is short: Carbyne had about ten potential buyers, maybe five realistic, and he knew what they pay. At $1.5–2B Carbyne would have had to IPO, which its product set at the time couldn't support ([[episodes/yasmins-living-room--amir-elichai-carbyne]], [[people/amir-elichai]]).
- Signal the window: he hired Goldman Sachs to tell the companies he cared about that Carbyne was raising $100M, so "if you're thinking of buying, now is the time". Bankers usually come in only at this stage ([[episodes/yasmins-living-room--amir-elichai-carbyne]], [[people/amir-elichai]]).
- The acquirer as investor: Axon's corporate development head said they don't buy companies at "day one" but offered to lead the round. Carbyne took Axon in as an investor, then got a letter of intent after two board meetings ([[episodes/yasmins-living-room--amir-elichai-carbyne]], [[people/amir-elichai]]).
- Win-win sell point: don't wait for the highest point on the chart (say $1.4B), where it either falls or becomes too expensive; sell where the buyer still has enough value to create afterwards ([[episodes/yasmins-living-room--amir-elichai-carbyne]], [[people/amir-elichai]]).
- Opportunity cost, "a bird in the hand": selling now versus a possible 20-year commitment to the company ([[episodes/yasmins-living-room--amir-elichai-carbyne]], [[people/amir-elichai]]).
- Mechanics: six weeks from LOI to the announcement at Axon's earnings, used to raise the offer from $550M cash + $75M equity to $625M cash + ~$100M equity; due diligence with ~80 lawyers and accountants; about three months between signing and closing ([[episodes/yasmins-living-room--amir-elichai-carbyne]], [[people/amir-elichai]]).
- Leverage comes from the fallback: near profitability, ~$30M ARR, $150–170M in bookings, long-term contracts with ~300 governments, and the buyer knew it ([[episodes/yasmins-living-room--amir-elichai-carbyne]], [[people/amir-elichai]]). Yasmin calls this the BATNA ([[episodes/yasmins-living-room--amir-elichai-carbyne]], [[people/yasmin]]).

## Disagreements & open questions
- Sell early or build bigger? Elichai argues for selling at a win-win point rather than the peak, yet expects his next company to be "10x bigger in half the time". The episode doesn't say how a founder knows they've reached that point ([[episodes/yasmins-living-room--amir-elichai-carbyne]], [[people/amir-elichai]]).

## Takeaways
- [ ] Before each raise, list who could still afford to buy you at the new valuation ([[episodes/yasmins-living-room--amir-elichai-carbyne]])
- [ ] If an acquisition is a realistic exit, let likely buyers know when you're about to raise ([[episodes/yasmins-living-room--amir-elichai-carbyne]])
