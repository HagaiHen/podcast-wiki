# Market Timing

**Summary:** Trying to sell before a crash or wait for one before buying usually fails, even when the argument is well reasoned and data-backed. Nobody, however smart, knows whether a bubble pops in three months or thirteen years, and the bottom of a future crash can still be higher than today's price, while you miss dividends on the way. A year's returns also cluster in a handful of exceptional days, in stocks and crypto alike, and nobody knows in advance which ones they'll be. Charts flatter hindsight: a buy at the exact low looks obvious afterward, but almost nobody can know it was the low, and even someone arguing that a selloff is a buying opportunity has to concede that. What you can control is your time horizon and your volatility tolerance; diversification, "the only free lunch", handles the rest.

## Key ideas
- Even if a crash comes in three years, its bottom might be ~8,000 on the S&P 500, above the ~7,722 at recording, and you'd also have missed the dividends ([[episodes/lazy-investing--111-the-ai-bubble]], [[people/tamir]]).
- Case study: a well-reasoned November 2024 post by a known financial blogger argued the S&P 500 was very expensive. Someone who pulled ₪100k then missed ~11% in shekels over two years (~30% in dollars including dividends, the gap due to the shekel's sharp rise) ([[episodes/lazy-investing--111-the-ai-bubble]], [[people/tamir]]).
- Selling an Israeli apartment in 2014 because prices "had peaked" missed six more years of strong gains ([[episodes/lazy-investing--111-the-ai-bubble]], [[people/tamir]]).
- The only things you know are your investment horizon and your tolerance for volatility; those, not forecasts, should drive decisions ([[episodes/lazy-investing--111-the-ai-bubble]], [[people/tamir]]).
- Diversification across companies *and* sectors is the only free lunch (Markowitz), unlike South Korean leveraged funds on a single company ([[episodes/lazy-investing--111-the-ai-bubble]], [[people/tamir]]).
- In both stocks and crypto there are on average ~10 exceptionally strong days a year; you never know which they are, or whether this year's have already happened ([[episodes/micha-stocks--bitcoin-uptober]], [[people/micha-stocks]]).
- Hindsight trap: buying Meta at its $537 low and holding to $779 would have made 44%, but the chance anyone knew that was the bottom "approaches zero". Someone who bought at its latest low of $609 may have caught another bottom, or it may fall further ([[episodes/micha-stocks--feels-like-bear-market]], [[people/micha-stocks]]).
- His alternative to calling the exact bottom: use a quiet weekend to decide what you'd want to do with these stocks, since he can't say whether now is too early or whether you should wait. He stresses this isn't advice to go and buy on Monday ([[episodes/micha-stocks--feels-like-bear-market]], [[people/micha-stocks]]).

## Disagreements & open questions
- Is buying a selloff a form of timing? Micha says bottoms can't be timed, yet calls this a great opportunity because "when everyone is negative, it's the time to be positive" ([[episodes/micha-stocks--feels-like-bear-market]], [[people/micha-stocks]]). Tamir says only your horizon and volatility tolerance, not forecasts, should drive decisions ([[episodes/lazy-investing--111-the-ai-bubble]], [[people/tamir]]).

## Takeaways
- [ ] Don't sell or delay investing because of bubble talk; decide by horizon and volatility tolerance ([[episodes/lazy-investing--111-the-ai-bubble]])
- [ ] In a selloff, separate companies with intact results and no warnings from those cutting guidance, and accept you won't catch the low ([[episodes/micha-stocks--feels-like-bear-market]])
