# Biohacking (N=1 self-experimentation)

**Summary:** Biohacking means running experiments on yourself, using your own genetic profile and measurable phenotype to draw conclusions that population studies can't. Within one person, age, sex, and genetics stay constant. Cheap sensors (continuous glucose monitors, ketone meters) and LLM-assisted analysis made it accessible to anyone with statistical literacy. The method has real weaknesses: no blinding or placebo, time dependence between trials, and chemical carry-over. It's best treated as hypothesis generation that informs your doctor, not as proof.

## Key ideas
- Bryan Johnson (Kernel; sold Braintree to PayPal) is the extreme: weekly blood tests, strict diet and exercise, early waking, and a biological-age reduction of about 10 years by objective measures. That's not a model to copy; most biohackers stop at supplements, nutrition, and training ([[episodes/explainable--152-biohacking-like-a-data-scientist]]).
- Professional athletes already do this (heart rate, blood pressure, glucose); CGMs show sugar spikes before and after meals ([[episodes/explainable--152-biohacking-like-a-data-scientist]]).
- Sensors costing a few hundred shekels let you verify a fast is working: glucose drops and ketones rise. The host does a ~40-hour fast weekly ([[episodes/explainable--152-biohacking-like-a-data-scientist]]).
- N=1 example: running with nose-only vs mouth-only breathing, where heart rate is lower with nasal breathing (a known effect) ([[episodes/explainable--152-biohacking-like-a-data-scientist]]).
- Critiques: the experimenter is both researcher and subject, so the placebo effect is strong; trials are sequential, so the body may change between them; a supplement taken for a month may not wash out ([[episodes/explainable--152-biohacking-like-a-data-scientist]]).
- Medicine is fragmented by specialty; a patient with broader genetic context and time can steer specialists in the right direction ([[episodes/explainable--152-biohacking-like-a-data-scientist]]).

## Disagreements & open questions
- Is N=1 evidence meaningful? The host acknowledges it can't control placebo or time effects, yet values it for personal understanding rather than as general proof ([[episodes/explainable--152-biohacking-like-a-data-scientist]]).

## Takeaways
- [ ] Try a CGM or ketone meter for a few weeks to see how your body actually responds to meals or fasting ([[episodes/explainable--152-biohacking-like-a-data-scientist]])
- [ ] For a self-experiment, alternate conditions more than once (A-B-A-B) to reduce time effects ([[episodes/explainable--152-biohacking-like-a-data-scientist]])
