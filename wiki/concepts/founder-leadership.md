# Founder Leadership

**Summary:** The founder-CEO's core job isn't being the best engineer, marketer or product person in the room but persuading people to follow: co-founders, employees, investors and customers. That makes the role a constant negotiation and sale. A non-technical founder can start by recruiting technical co-founders around a real problem, and leadership learned as a combat officer (motivating people, setting red lines, working under pressure) carries over. Self-belief helps when it is earned through small wins, not "in the clouds", and the CEO's personal story carries the company's brand. Focus on one market beats early expansion.

## Key ideas
- A CEO is "always selling" and negotiating, 24/7: with co-founders, employees, investors, the board and customers, and over product priorities ([[episodes/yasmins-living-room--amir-elichai-carbyne]], [[people/amir-elichai]]).
- You don't have to be the smartest technologist or marketer; you have to get everyone moving together, set goals and reach them ([[episodes/yasmins-living-room--amir-elichai-carbyne]], [[people/amir-elichai]]).
- Non-technical founders: his first act of persuasion was getting two technologists (Alex Dizengof and Yoni) to join, which turned an idea and a deck into a POC, a pilot and something to show investors ([[episodes/yasmins-living-room--amir-elichai-carbyne]], [[people/amir-elichai]]).
- Combat officers learn leadership, work under pressure, motivating subordinates and setting red lines, which carries "enormous weight" for a startup CEO. He suggests teams that pair combat veterans with technologists ([[episodes/yasmins-living-room--amir-elichai-carbyne]], [[people/amir-elichai]]).
- Self-efficacy comes from a chain of good choices and small successes that build realistic confidence ([[episodes/yasmins-living-room--amir-elichai-carbyne]], [[people/amir-elichai]]). Yasmin notes that studies of successful founders stress belief in yourself regardless of ability ([[episodes/yasmins-living-room--amir-elichai-carbyne]], [[people/yasmin]]).
- Competitiveness and "winner-ness" are what investors look for, but confidence should sit in the middle of the bell curve, not at the extremes ([[episodes/yasmins-living-room--amir-elichai-carbyne]], [[people/amir-elichai]]).
- Be honest about fit: entrepreneurship is a 0-or-1 journey with many failures nobody writes about. Some people, after mapping their goals, are better off in a corporate job; if you'd dread being CEO every morning, it shows to employees and investors ([[episodes/yasmins-living-room--amir-elichai-carbyne]], [[people/amir-elichai]]).
- Brand and storytelling matter even when selling to governments: Carbyne renamed itself from "Reporty" (it sounded like an app) and spent about $0.5M with Pentagram on a brand ahead of its Series C, which he says paid off with investors, hires and the public ([[episodes/yasmins-living-room--amir-elichai-carbyne]], [[people/amir-elichai]]).
- Focus: in hindsight he'd have gone straight to the US instead of spreading into South America. An investor with 40,000 employees told him they focus only on the US because there's still room to grow there ([[episodes/yasmins-living-room--amir-elichai-carbyne]], [[people/amir-elichai]]).
- Business culture differs: the Emirates take their time and are thorough, while in the US no follow-up meeting booked means no interest. People do business with people, so learn what each counterpart wants (re-election, a promotion) ([[episodes/yasmins-living-room--amir-elichai-carbyne]], [[people/amir-elichai]]).
- Work-life: quality over hours; be fully at the office or fully at home, and take weekends and holidays so people come back more focused ([[episodes/yasmins-living-room--amir-elichai-carbyne]], [[people/amir-elichai]]).

## Disagreements & open questions
- Can it be learned? Elichai says the always-selling trait is "in your personality" and hard to learn, while his own account credits upbringing and accumulated choices ([[episodes/yasmins-living-room--amir-elichai-carbyne]], [[people/amir-elichai]]).

## Takeaways
- [ ] If you're non-technical with a validated problem, pitch one technologist to join before pitching investors ([[episodes/yasmins-living-room--amir-elichai-carbyne]])
- [ ] Pick one target market and stay in it until it works before expanding ([[episodes/yasmins-living-room--amir-elichai-carbyne]])
