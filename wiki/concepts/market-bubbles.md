# Market Bubbles

**Summary:** Bubbles are a normal, human part of every big technology wave: railways, cars, credit cards, email, the dot-com internet, and now AI. Investor expectations rise for a reason that is partly justified, then overshoot, and anything with the hot label attracts money out of fear of missing out. Whether the 2026 AI boom is one is contested: one view points to debt-funded capex with little profit yet, extreme index concentration, and a CAPE last seen in the dot-com era; the other notes that tech now trades at roughly the S&P 500's P/E, versus a 100%+ premium in 2000. The weak point is the private frontier labs: their valuations rest on profitability that keeps being pushed out (2027 → 2029), and Anthropic's IPO filing pairs explosive revenue growth with large losses and a target of hundreds of times last year's revenue. Either way, a bubble can run for years before it pops, and for a long-term index investor a crash is a chance to buy cheaper, not a reason to stay out.

## Key ideas
- Since ChatGPT's launch, AI stocks account for ~75% of S&P 500 returns, ~80% of earnings growth, and ~90% of the growth in capital investment ([[episodes/lazy-investing--111-the-ai-bubble]], [[people/tamir]]).
- Big tech invests huge, partly leveraged sums in AI with little profit so far, because not investing means being left behind the way Nokia was by Apple; that leverage on high development costs is genuinely risky and could end in a burst ([[episodes/lazy-investing--111-the-ai-bubble]], [[people/tamir]]).
- Fast-moving tech makes past investment obsolete: what a team learned two or three years ago can become irrelevant when a new model ships ([[episodes/lazy-investing--111-the-ai-bubble]]).
- Bubbles recur with every productivity leap (steam engine and railways, Fordism, credit cards, email), and the internet was a bubble in 1999, yet investing in 1999 was still an excellent long-term decision because the internet ended up lifting all 500 companies in the index ([[episodes/lazy-investing--111-the-ai-bubble]], [[people/tamir]]).
- You don't need AI ETFs to benefit from AI: "boring" companies (retailers, grocers, dividend aristocrats) adopt it too; the host's six-person company now writes a landing page in five minutes instead of paying ~₪7,000 ([[episodes/lazy-investing--111-the-ai-bubble]], [[people/tamir]]).
- A long-horizon investor (20+ years) should *want* a bubble to burst, to buy the same stocks cheaply ([[episodes/lazy-investing--111-the-ai-bubble]], [[people/tamir]]).
- In 1999–2000 tech traded at a P/E around 55 against ~25 for the S&P 500, a 100%+ premium; in 2026 tech's P/E is about the same as the index's, so "there is no bubble" by that measure, even though prices can still fall ([[episodes/micha-stocks--index-bull-portfolio-bear]], [[people/micha-stocks]]).
- Nvidia's multiple was cut roughly in half on fears it overspent on capacity; the host calls those fears, not reality ([[episodes/micha-stocks--index-bull-portfolio-bear]], [[people/micha-stocks]]).
- Anthropic's S-1: H1 2026 revenue of $16.3B (from $1.1B a year earlier) but huge compute losses, and an IPO target of $1.5–2T, ~327× 2025 revenue. "Very, very expensive", which doesn't say buy or don't buy; remember the bills it already owes its cloud providers ([[episodes/micha-stocks--anthropic-s1-numbers]], [[people/micha-stocks]]).
- Lab profitability is the hinge: no lab has shown it's profitable, forecasts moved from 2027 to 2029, and if they don't get there "the whole bubble can burst", taking pensions and savings with it ([[episodes/explainable--167-yuval-avidani-agent-harness]], [[people/yuval-avidani]]).

## Disagreements & open questions
- Is the AI boom a bubble? The host says it's not yet proven: it may be the early days of a decade-long rally, or it may pop next year ([[episodes/lazy-investing--111-the-ai-bubble]]).
- Tamir sees the AI boom's signs as a possible bubble that could burst ([[episodes/lazy-investing--111-the-ai-bubble]]); Micha says zero tech valuation premium means no bubble ([[episodes/micha-stocks--index-bull-portfolio-bear]]).
