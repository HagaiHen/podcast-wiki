# Market Bubbles

**Summary:** Bubbles are a normal, human part of every big technology wave: railways, cars, credit cards, email, the dot-com internet, and now AI. Investor expectations rise for a reason that is partly justified, then overshoot, and anything with the hot label attracts money out of fear of missing out. Whether the 2026 AI boom is one is contested: one view points to debt-funded capex with little profit yet, extreme index concentration, and a CAPE last seen in the dot-com era; the other notes that tech now trades at roughly the S&P 500's P/E, versus a 100%+ premium in 2000. The weak point is the private frontier labs: their valuations rest on profitability that keeps being pushed out (2027 → 2029), and Anthropic's IPO filing pairs explosive revenue growth with large losses and a target of hundreds of times last year's revenue. Sentiment can also swing the other way: by the end of October 2026 about $4T had left the public giants, which Micha read as AI being priced in for free. By mid-November defensive sectors led the Nasdaq by the widest gap in 25 years, a pattern last seen in late 2001; Micha reads that as a broad rotation, not proof that the AI bubble burst. Days later the Nasdaq entered a bear market and charts showed the AI trade erased: Nvidia had lost ~48% (about $2.8T) since early October amid a reported SEC accounting review and signs of margin pressure, and neoclouds were 70–85% below their highs. Micha still argues AI demand and profits haven't gone away. Either way, a bubble can run for years before it pops, and for a long-term index investor a crash is a chance to buy cheaper, not a reason to stay out.

## Key ideas
- Since ChatGPT's launch, AI stocks account for ~75% of S&P 500 returns, ~80% of earnings growth, and ~90% of the growth in capital investment ([[episodes/lazy-investing--111-the-ai-bubble]], [[people/tamir]]).
- Big tech invests huge, partly leveraged sums in AI with little profit so far, because not investing means being left behind the way Nokia was by Apple; that leverage on high development costs is genuinely risky and could end in a burst ([[episodes/lazy-investing--111-the-ai-bubble]], [[people/tamir]]).
- Fast-moving tech makes past investment obsolete: what a team learned two or three years ago can become irrelevant when a new model ships ([[episodes/lazy-investing--111-the-ai-bubble]]).
- Bubbles recur with every productivity leap (steam engine and railways, Fordism, credit cards, email), and the internet was a bubble in 1999, yet investing in 1999 was still an excellent long-term decision because the internet ended up lifting all 500 companies in the index ([[episodes/lazy-investing--111-the-ai-bubble]], [[people/tamir]]).
- You don't need AI ETFs to benefit from AI: "boring" companies (retailers, grocers, dividend aristocrats) adopt it too; the host's six-person company now writes a landing page in five minutes instead of paying ~₪7,000 ([[episodes/lazy-investing--111-the-ai-bubble]], [[people/tamir]]).
- A long-horizon investor (20+ years) should *want* a bubble to burst, to buy the same stocks cheaply ([[episodes/lazy-investing--111-the-ai-bubble]], [[people/tamir]]).
- In 1999–2000 tech traded at a P/E around 55 against ~25 for the S&P 500, a 100%+ premium; in 2026 tech's P/E is about the same as the index's, so "there is no bubble" by that measure, even though prices can still fall ([[episodes/micha-stocks--index-bull-portfolio-bear]], [[people/micha-stocks]]).
- Nvidia's multiple was cut roughly in half on fears it overspent on capacity; the host calls those fears, not reality ([[episodes/micha-stocks--index-bull-portfolio-bear]], [[people/micha-stocks]]).
- Anthropic's S-1: H1 2026 revenue of $16.3B (from $1.1B a year earlier) but huge compute losses, and an IPO target of $1.5–2T, ~327× 2025 revenue. "Very, very expensive", which doesn't say buy or don't buy; remember the bills it already owes its cloud providers ([[episodes/micha-stocks--anthropic-s1-numbers]], [[people/micha-stocks]]).
- Lab profitability is the hinge: no lab has shown it's profitable, forecasts moved from 2027 to 2029, and if they don't get there "the whole bubble can burst", taking pensions and savings with it ([[episodes/explainable--167-yuval-avidani-agent-harness]], [[people/yuval-avidani]]).
- About $4T moved out of big tech into other stocks in a few weeks. Micha calls that roughly AI's whole valuation, so AI is now "priced for free" in those stocks and the drop looks overdone ([[episodes/micha-stocks--feels-like-bear-market]], [[people/micha-stocks]]).
- The market is pricing the infrastructure bill: heavy capex means negative cash flow, which it reads forward as lower future profits, although he says EPS isn't falling and the giants haven't warned about coming quarters. Intel, which fell 30% in a day, is his contrast ([[episodes/micha-stocks--feels-like-bear-market]], [[people/micha-stocks]]).
- Rotation, not a burst: Morgan Stanley's derivatives desk found that from October 1 to November 17 the S&P 500's defensive sectors (consumer staples, healthcare, utilities) rose 13% while the Nasdaq fell 13%. A 26% gap hadn't been seen in 25 years, since September–October 2001. Micha's reading: if you think the recent drops are the AI bubble bursting, "it's not related at all"; they look like part of a broader move ([[episodes/micha-stocks--nvidia-beats-without-jensen]], [[people/micha-stocks]]).
- The AI trade erased: on November 20, 2026 the Nasdaq entered a bear market, and "on a lot of charts it's as if the AI trade never happened" ([[episodes/micha-stocks--nasdaq-bear-market]], [[people/micha-stocks]]).
- Nvidia as "the bank of AI": it fell 13.8% in a day, 22.7% in a week and ~48% since October 6, 2026, a record ~$2.8T loss of market cap. Triggers: a Reuters report that the SEC is examining possible accounting problems, and investor Gavin Baker saying portfolio companies see competitors trying to take Nvidia's business while Nvidia offers better terms, which could hurt its margins. Because Nvidia also funds many AI companies, its fall weighs on the whole group ([[episodes/micha-stocks--nasdaq-bear-market]], [[people/micha-stocks]]).
- Anthropic (ticker ANT) fell another ~9% in its second trading day, and Micha said nobody who had bought it was yet in profit (garbled transcript, approximate) ([[episodes/micha-stocks--nasdaq-bear-market]], [[people/micha-stocks]]).

## Disagreements & open questions
- Is the AI boom a bubble? The host says it's not yet proven: it may be the early days of a decade-long rally, or it may pop next year ([[episodes/lazy-investing--111-the-ai-bubble]]).
- Tamir sees the AI boom's signs as a possible bubble that could burst ([[episodes/lazy-investing--111-the-ai-bubble]]); Micha says zero tech valuation premium means no bubble ([[episodes/micha-stocks--index-bull-portfolio-bear]]).
- Priced for free or overpriced? Micha now says the public giants have shed AI's value ([[episodes/micha-stocks--feels-like-bear-market]]); he also calls Anthropic's IPO target very expensive ([[episodes/micha-stocks--anthropic-s1-numbers]]), and Tamir warns that big tech's leveraged AI spending could end in a burst ([[episodes/lazy-investing--111-the-ai-bubble]]). Micha's two views can coexist, since they concern different assets.
- Burst or overreaction? By November 20 the market had erased the AI trade and Nvidia had lost about half its value; Micha's reading is that the AI trade isn't over, since companies keep buying and profiting from AI ([[episodes/micha-stocks--nasdaq-bear-market]]). That is the same claim he made days earlier about a rotation ([[episodes/micha-stocks--nvidia-beats-without-jensen]]), now tested by a much deeper fall.

## Takeaways
- [ ] Get AI exposure through broad indexes rather than AI-themed or leveraged AI ETFs ([[episodes/lazy-investing--111-the-ai-bubble]])
- [ ] Before calling a tech selloff an AI-bubble burst, compare defensive sectors with the Nasdaq over the same period ([[episodes/micha-stocks--nvidia-beats-without-jensen]])
