# Cost of Living (competition, subsidies, price controls)

**Summary:** Cost of living is income, minus taxes, against prices. Firms charge the maximum the market lets them, so prices that rose with a weak currency don't fall when it strengthens unless competition forces them down; without continuous effort to create competition, cartels and monopolies form naturally. Two Israeli candidates interviewed on the same show agree that competition is the lever but stress different tools. Naftali Bennett warns that cash subsidies get captured by sellers and price caps cause shortages, and prefers target-price mechanisms. Yaron Zelicha targets import barriers: abolish tariffs and quotas, bar local monopolies from importing in their own category, split import cartels, and actually enforce competition law. He claims Israelis overpay ~35%, and that the freed-up income would fund VAT cuts through growth. Whether more disposable income just feeds inflation and housing prices is disputed.

## Key ideas
- Israeli price levels (PPP) rose ~35% over 15 years because competition stalled; prices are "the maximum I can take", so blame the government that allows it, not the firms ([[episodes/micha-stocks--naftali-bennett-economic-interview]], [[people/naftali-bennett]]).
- Example: Tnuva raised prices three times when the shekel weakened at the start of the war, then didn't cut them when it strengthened ([[episodes/micha-stocks--naftali-bennett-economic-interview]], [[people/naftali-bennett]]).
- Price caps with limited supply (e.g. airline seats) make who gets served nearly random; people lose not on price but on access. Price control usually ends in shortage ([[episodes/micha-stocks--naftali-bennett-economic-interview]], [[people/naftali-bennett]]).
- Subsidy capture: a cash grant per buyer lets sellers raise prices by the grant (daycare, or contractors adding ₪1M to apartments) ([[episodes/micha-stocks--naftali-bennett-economic-interview]], [[people/micha-stocks]]).
- Bennett's counter: work backward from a target price (like Israel's *Mechir LaMishtaken* subsidized-housing lottery) instead of writing checks ([[episodes/micha-stocks--naftali-bennett-economic-interview]], [[people/naftali-bennett]]).
- Housing supply ideas: incentivize developers to build long-term rentals (defer VAT to handover, state guarantees to banks), neighborhood-scale urban renewal, and incentives for mayors, since residents cost a city money while commerce brings it in ([[episodes/micha-stocks--naftali-bennett-economic-interview]], [[people/naftali-bennett]]). See [[concepts/housing-prices]].
- Banks keep developers from cutting nominal apartment prices, so prices fall hidden in promotions; Bennett calls it a time bomb that needs a controlled decline ([[episodes/micha-stocks--naftali-bennett-economic-interview]], [[people/naftali-bennett]]).
- Cost of living and housing are "twins": you can't fix one without the other. The root cause is a closed economy where barriers stop competitors from playing fairly ([[episodes/micha-stocks--yaron-zelicha-economic-interview]], [[people/yaron-zelicha]]).
- Monopolies created by regulation, not genius: Metro Motor dominates motorcycles because the Transport Ministry gave it exclusive import licenses for Yamaha, Kawasaki and SYM long enough to become exclusive de facto. "If you import 80–90% of Yamaha, you're not an importer, you're Yamaha" ([[episodes/micha-stocks--yaron-zelicha-economic-interview]], [[people/yaron-zelicha]]).
- Local-production monopolies (Tnuva, Strauss, Osem) also import in their own categories, using power built partly on old government subsidies to block import competition ([[episodes/micha-stocks--yaron-zelicha-economic-interview]], [[people/yaron-zelicha]]).
- Zelicha's five first-budget steps: (1) abolish all tariffs, which bring negligible revenue and exist to block imports; (2) abolish import quotas; (3) a monopoly in local production may not import in that category; (4) anyone importing over 50% of a major international maker is its distributor and can't import competing brands; (5) move the Competition Authority under the Finance Ministry so the law is enforced. Competition law is good on paper but weakly enforced by international measures, and authority heads often go on to work for the monopolies ([[episodes/micha-stocks--yaron-zelicha-economic-interview]], [[people/yaron-zelicha]]).
- "Robbery" estimate: of ~₪1 trillion net private income, ~₪250B is overpayment, i.e. prices ~35% above where they should be ([[episodes/micha-stocks--yaron-zelicha-economic-interview]], [[people/yaron-zelicha]]).
- Funding tax cuts with growth: freed income gets spent (mostly at small businesses), raising growth, and each 1% of growth raises tax revenue ~1.25%. He wants VAT at 12% within six years, citing the 2003–2005 cut from 18% to 15% while the deficit was double today's ([[episodes/micha-stocks--yaron-zelicha-economic-interview]], [[people/yaron-zelicha]]).
- He separates organized labor from monopolies: he backs workers facing stronger employers but opposes monopoly unions (e.g. at the Airports Authority) that use their power to block competition ([[episodes/micha-stocks--yaron-zelicha-economic-interview]], [[people/yaron-zelicha]]).

## Disagreements & open questions
- Can targeted subsidies avoid inflation? The host fears grants will just raise daycare and apartment prices; Bennett says routing them through target-price mechanisms prevents that. Untested campaign claim ([[episodes/micha-stocks--naftali-bennett-economic-interview]]).
- Does more disposable income cause inflation? Micha argues cheaper living plus growth puts more money in households' hands and pushes prices and housing up; Zelicha says that only happens at potential output, and Israel is far below it (~1% per-capita growth vs ~4% potential), so the output gap absorbs it ([[episodes/micha-stocks--yaron-zelicha-economic-interview]], [[people/micha-stocks]], [[people/yaron-zelicha]]).
- Micha calls the plan's "how" too easy and populist; Zelicha says it's detailed in a multi-page program. The host also pressed him to disclose his consulting clients; he says none were monopolies and he'll disclose them on taking office (campaign claims, [[episodes/micha-stocks--yaron-zelicha-economic-interview]]).
