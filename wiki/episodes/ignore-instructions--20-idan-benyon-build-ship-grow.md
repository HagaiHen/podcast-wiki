# #20 — The Man Behind the AI Builders Community — with Idan Benyon

> Transcript note: two garbled stretches (one on "shipping", one on how to pick a first idea) are lost.

[[people/idan-benyon]] founded Build Ship Grow, a WhatsApp/LinkedIn community of AI side-project builders that grew from a few friends to thousands in about 18 months, and spun off groups such as Squid Club.
He describes a widening gap between these communities and big tech: at Apple Haifa most engineers had used Claude Code, half knew sub-agents, about three used skills.
The new "builder" profile: domain experts and go-to-market people who build products cheaply without VC money, such as a jeweler's 3D design tool on Base44, or a creator with 510k Arabic-language AI followers running several ~$15k-MRR products. That calls for new, non-VC financing models.
Distribution is the bottleneck when software is 10× cheaper. Tactics: build in public on LinkedIn, WhatsApp beta groups, Reddit, AI-generated TikTok content engines, and open-sourcing the product.
On shipping: release early, get feedback from widening circles, and finish what you start. Host Eitan (Mixtiles) adds his own lesson of shipping bad podcast episodes and improving. Infrastructure trends: durable objects and sandboxes for long-running agents, and shareable assistant "skill packages".
